## Item 8: which Carruthers to cite

**Recommendation: `carruthers2011opacity`.**

- Its OUP abstract says access to one's own thoughts is "almost always interpretive", which is closest to "often inferred".
- It is the later and complete statement of the view.
- Its chapters include "Metacognition and Control", "Dissociation Data" and "Self-Interpretation and Confabulation". I checked the chapter titles but did not read the chapter text.

**Caveat for the sentence in the paper.** Both works restrict the claim to propositional attitudes (thoughts, judgments, decisions). Carruthers holds that perceptual and quasi-perceptual states, such as imagery and inner speech, can be introspected. The safer wording is "knowledge of one's own thoughts and decisions is often (Carruthers argues, almost always) inferred", not "one's own states".

**When to use the 2009 article instead.** Use it if you prefer a peer-reviewed article with page numbers and open commentary. It makes the same core claim, the "mindreading is prior" model: attitudes are always known by rapid, unconscious self-interpretation, and it uses confabulation evidence. Its verified entry:
